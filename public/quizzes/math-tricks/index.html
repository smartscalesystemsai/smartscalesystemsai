Mind-Blowing Math Tricks (Looks like Magic)

15 questions with answers and explanations · free to read and reuse

Mental arithmetic shortcuts look like sleight of hand and are mostly just structure. Squaring a number ending in five, multiplying by eleven, converting an awkward percentage into an easy one.

Each of these has a reason underneath it. The point is not to memorise the trick but to see the structure that makes it work — which is why the explanations show the mechanism rather than just the result.

Every question below shows all four options, the keyed answer, and the one-line explanation. If you want to turn these into video, the OmniFrame Quiz Studio is free and this pack is built in.

All 15 questions

Question 1

A calculator shows 101 - 10 = 11. It is not broken. What number system is it using?

  • Decimal
  • Binary  ← answer
  • Octal
  • Hexadecimal

Why: In binary, 101 is 5, 10 is 2, and 11 is 3, so the equation is correct.

Question 2

A number is 20% larger than another. By what percent is the second number smaller than the first?

  • 20%
  • 25%
  • 16.67%  ← answer
  • 80%

Why: If the smaller number is 100, the larger is 120. The drop is 20 out of 120.

Question 3

You double a number, add 10, divide by 2, and subtract the original number. Why is the answer always 5?

  • The number becomes zero first
  • Ten is ignored
  • Division doubles the number
  • The original cancels out  ← answer

Why: The expression becomes (2x+10)/2 - x, which simplifies to 5.

Question 4

Which is better: a 30% discount then 20% discount, or 50% discount once?

  • 50% once  ← answer
  • 30% then 20%
  • Both give 50%
  • It depends on the original price

Why: 30% then 20% leaves 56% of the price, so the discount is 44%.

Question 5

A clock gains 10 minutes hourly and reads 3 PM after six real hours. What did it display at the start?

  • 7:50 AM
  • 8:00 AM  ← answer
  • 9:00 AM
  • 1:50 PM

Why: In six real hours the clock advances seven displayed hours, so a 3 PM reading began at 8 AM.

Question 6

Multiply any nonzero whole number by 9, then repeatedly sum its digits. What number remains?

  • 3
  • 6
  • 9  ← answer
  • 18

Why: Every positive multiple of 9 has digital root 9.

Question 7

What is 25% of 80, and why does the reverse trick work?

  • 15
  • 25
  • 40
  • 20  ← answer

Why: 25% of 80 equals 80% of 25, which is easier: 20.

Question 8

What is 15% of 200 without a calculator?

  • 30  ← answer
  • 20
  • 25
  • 35

Why: 10% is 20 and 5% is 10, so together they make 30.

Question 9

What is 11 times 23 using the quick middle-digit trick?

  • 233
  • 253  ← answer
  • 243
  • 263

Why: For 23 x 11, keep 2 and 3 outside, then put 2+3 in the middle.

Question 10

What is 99 x 47 using the shortcut?

  • 4606
  • 4653  ← answer
  • 4693
  • 4747

Why: 100 x 47 is 4700. Subtract 47 to get 4653.

Question 11

What is the square of 35 using the ending-in-5 shortcut?

  • 1025
  • 1125
  • 1225  ← answer
  • 1325

Why: 3 x 4 gives 12, then add 25.

Question 12

Which is bigger: 40% of 70 or 70% of 40?

  • 40% of 70
  • 70% of 40
  • Cannot be compared
  • They are equal  ← answer

Why: Percent multiplication can swap order: 0.4 x 70 equals 0.7 x 40.

Question 13

What number becomes larger when you turn it upside down?

  • 6  ← answer
  • 5
  • 7
  • 8

Why: Turn a 6 upside down and it becomes 9.

Question 14

What is 50% of 2 plus 2?

  • 2
  • 3  ← answer
  • 4
  • 6

Why: 50% of 2 is 1, then add 2.

Question 15

A number doubled and then doubled again becomes 64. What was it?

  • 8
  • 24
  • 16  ← answer
  • 32

Why: Doubling twice means multiplying by 4, so 64 divided by 4 is 16.
