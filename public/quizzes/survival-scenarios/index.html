Survival & 'What Would You Do?' Scenarios

15 questions with answers and explanations · free to read and reuse

Survival advice circulates widely and a good deal of it is wrong. Moss does not reliably indicate north. Berries eaten by birds are not therefore safe for you.

These questions target decisions where the intuitive move is actively dangerous — swimming against a rip current, putting water on a grease fire, sheltering under the tallest nearby tree. Worth reading as general knowledge rather than as training; none of it substitutes for actual instruction.

Every question below shows all four options, the keyed answer, and the one-line explanation. If you want to turn these into video, the OmniFrame Quiz Studio is free and this pack is built in.

All 15 questions

Question 1

You are lost with 10% phone battery. What should you do before recording videos?

  • Record video continuously
  • Increase screen brightness
  • Send location and conserve battery  ← answer
  • Stream music

Why: Communication and location matter before nonessential use.

Question 2

A river looks calm but is moving fast below. What danger is hidden?

  • Calm water has no danger
  • Only cold temperature
  • A harmless reflection
  • Current  ← answer

Why: Surface calm can hide strong underwater movement.

Question 3

You smell gas in a building. What should you avoid first?

  • Switches and flames  ← answer
  • Opening a window
  • Leaving the area
  • Calling emergency services from outside

Why: Sparks or flames can ignite gas.

Question 4

You are caught in a rip current. What direction should you swim?

  • Directly against the current
  • Parallel to shore  ← answer
  • Straight toward shore
  • Farther out to sea

Why: Swimming parallel helps escape the narrow current.

Question 5

You find berries in the wild and birds eat them. Are they safe?

  • Yes, always
  • Yes, if they are red
  • Not guaranteed  ← answer
  • Yes, after one bird eats them

Why: Animals can tolerate foods humans cannot.

Question 6

In desert heat, which is safer: rushing at midday or conserving water while resting in shade?

  • Speed
  • Carry extra weight
  • Travel at midday
  • Conserve water in shade  ← answer

Why: Reducing exertion and heat exposure while conserving water lowers dehydration and heat-illness risk.

Question 7

If your clothes get wet in freezing weather, what is the priority?

  • Get dry and insulated  ← answer
  • Keep the wet clothes on
  • Exercise until exhausted
  • Lie on bare snow

Why: Wet clothing pulls heat away from the body.

Question 8

What universal signal often means distress?

  • One long signal
  • Three repeated signals  ← answer
  • Two random signals
  • Four silent signals

Why: Three fires, whistles, or flashes are commonly used for distress.

Question 9

If you are lost, why might staying put help?

  • You become invisible
  • It guarantees rescue immediately
  • Rescuers can find you  ← answer
  • Weather cannot reach you

Why: Moving randomly can expand the search area.

Question 10

What should you do before drinking unknown water?

  • Drink it immediately
  • Judge safety by clarity
  • Purify it  ← answer
  • Add salt only

Why: Boiling, filtering, or treating reduces illness risk.

Question 11

What is the first rule around a downed power line?

  • Touch it with dry wood
  • Drive over it
  • Move it yourself
  • Stay away  ← answer

Why: Downed lines can energize the ground.

Question 12

During lightning, which shelter is especially dangerous?

  • Under an isolated tree  ← answer
  • Inside a substantial building
  • In a hard-topped vehicle
  • Inside an underground station

Why: An isolated tree can attract a strike and expose a nearby person to side flash and ground current.

Question 13

What is the safest first step with a small grease fire?

  • Throw water on it
  • Cut heat and cover it  ← answer
  • Carry the pan outside
  • Fan the flames

Why: Water can spread burning grease.

Question 14

If someone is bleeding heavily, what helps first?

  • Wash the wound for ten minutes first
  • Remove embedded objects
  • Direct pressure  ← answer
  • Wait without applying pressure

Why: Pressure slows bleeding until help arrives.

Question 15

Can moss direction reliably tell you which way is north?

  • Always north
  • Always south
  • Always east
  • No  ← answer

Why: Moss growth depends on local moisture, shade, and surface conditions, so it is not a reliable compass.
