True Crime & Unsolved Mysteries

15 questions with answers and explanations · free to read and reuse

Investigation, stripped of the atmosphere, is mostly reconciling timelines and noticing what does not fit. A receipt with a timestamp. Handwriting where every letter is identical.

Several of these questions point deliberately at the failure modes, because those are more instructive than the successes. Eyewitness memory is far less reliable than intuition suggests, particularly under stress, and it is highly suggestible — the phrasing of a question can alter what a witness subsequently remembers seeing.

The other trap is the compelling clue. A single piece of evidence that seems to explain everything tends to reorganize an investigation around itself, and contradicting evidence gets discounted rather than examined. The strength of a theory is not how well it explains the evidence you noticed; it is how it holds up against the evidence you were inclined to ignore.

Worth reading as reasoning practice rather than as an accurate picture of how any real case was solved.

Every question below shows all four options, the keyed answer, and the one-line explanation. If you want to turn these into video, the OmniFrame Quiz Studio is free and this pack is built in.
